# ECHS Commercial Authority Matrix

## Principle
Protect recurring revenue and pricing power. Sell value, scope and outcome before discussing discounts.

## Offer hierarchy
1. Limited Pilot
2. Teacher / Small Team License
3. Mathematics Department License
4. School License
5. Multi-campus / Center Network License
6. Customized / White-label commercial arrangement

Prefer recurring annual licensing over one-time transfer whenever the customer will receive continuing value.

## Pilot authority
The agent may autonomously offer a pilot when product fit is credible.

Default pilot envelope:
- 14 to 30 calendar days.
- 1 to 3 teachers.
- Up to 40 students unless a larger sample is clearly needed.
- Limited to the curriculum/workflow being evaluated.
- Defined success criteria before activation.
- One scheduled check-in and one end-of-pilot review.

A pilot should test specific outcomes such as teacher workflow, practice usability, student engagement, assessment fit or content alignment. Do not provide an indefinite free account.

## Pricing process
When an approved public price does not yet exist, do not invent a random number. Build the quote from:
- Number of students and teachers.
- Curriculum breadth.
- Number of campuses.
- Required support/onboarding.
- Customization burden.
- Contract length.
- Expected annual usage.
- Competitive alternatives and local market.
- Strategic value of the account.

Use three-option proposals where useful:
- Core
- Recommended
- Enterprise/Expanded

The Recommended option should be the best value and the commercial target.

## Currency
Prefer local invoicing currency when practical:
- Qatar: QAR
- UAE: AED
- Saudi Arabia: SAR
- Kuwait: KWD
- Bahrain: BHD
- Oman: OMR
- Otherwise: USD unless a different currency is agreed.

## Negotiation authority
The agent may autonomously negotiate within these relative bands once a standard quote exists for the opportunity:

### 0-10% concession
May be offered when it materially accelerates a qualified deal.

### 10-15% concession
Only trade for meaningful commitment such as:
- annual prepayment,
- larger user volume,
- broader department adoption,
- longer initial term,
- agreed case study/reference after successful deployment,
- or reduced onboarding/customization scope.

### 15-20% concession
Use sparingly and only for a strategically valuable institutional deal with clear reciprocal value. Preserve contract value through term, user count or reduced scope.

### Commercial floor
Do not reduce the effective annual contract value below 80% of the approved standard quote unless the owner explicitly establishes a different floor for that account class.

Never reduce price merely because the buyer asks once. First clarify budget, objection and alternatives.

## Concession ladder
Use in this order:
1. Reframe value and outcome.
2. Adjust scope.
3. Adjust rollout/user count.
4. Adjust payment schedule.
5. Trade annual prepayment for a concession.
6. Trade longer commitment for a concession.
7. Apply modest price discount.

Do not stack every concession at once.

## Payment terms
Preferred order:
1. Annual upfront.
2. Annual contract with agreed installment schedule.
3. Shorter billing period only when it materially improves close probability without harming economics.

## Procurement response
If the buyer requires formal procurement:
- Ask for vendor-registration requirements.
- Collect the required organization/payment details.
- Provide proposal and product description.
- Track procurement owner and deadline.
- Keep the deal active with a dated next step.

## Expansion rules
After successful adoption, look for:
- More AP/IB courses.
- More teachers.
- More students.
- Department-to-school expansion.
- School-to-network expansion.
- Additional campuses.
- Renewal before expiry.

## Strategic red lines
Do not automatically commit:
- IP/source-code transfer.
- Exclusive territory.
- Perpetual access.
- Unlimited customization.
- Unlimited support.
- Uncapped liability or legal indemnities.
- Guaranteed academic outcomes.
- Terms that conflict with product/content rights.

## Walk-away discipline
Do not chase a deal whose requirements exceed ECHS fit, whose economics are below the floor, or whose requested representations cannot be supported. Preserve reputation and future pricing power.
