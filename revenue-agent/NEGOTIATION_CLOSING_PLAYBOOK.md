# ECHS Negotiation & Closing Playbook

## Goal
Move qualified prospects from interest to a clear commercial decision while protecting value, credibility and long-term recurring revenue.

## Core sequence
DISCOVER -> QUANTIFY PAIN -> CONFIRM FIT -> DEMO -> PILOT/PROPOSAL -> NEGOTIATE -> CLOSE -> ONBOARD

## Discovery before price
Before quoting, establish as much as practical:
- program/curriculum,
- teachers/students/campuses,
- current workflow/tool,
- pain and urgency,
- decision maker,
- procurement process,
- budget signal,
- desired start date.

Do not force every question if the buyer is already ready to purchase.

## Negotiation model
Use: Acknowledge -> Clarify -> Quantify -> Reframe -> Trade -> Close.

### Price objection
1. Ask what the buyer is comparing the price against.
2. Clarify whether the constraint is total budget, timing, user count or perceived value.
3. Reframe around the relevant outcome and scope.
4. Reduce scope before reducing price when possible.
5. If a concession is necessary, use COMMERCIAL_AUTHORITY_MATRIX.md and trade it for commitment.

### "We already have an LMS/platform"
Do not attack the incumbent.
Ask whether it already provides the specialized advanced-math content/practice/question-bank workflow ECHS is being evaluated for. Position ECHS as complementary where appropriate.

### "Send information"
Send a short relevant summary, not a brochure dump. Include one CTA: demo, pilot, or short call.

### "Not now"
Determine whether the reason is calendar timing, budget cycle, curriculum planning or lack of fit. If timing, record a precise reactivation date. If no fit, close respectfully.

### "We need approval"
Ask who participates in the decision and what they need. Offer a concise forwardable summary or join the next decision meeting.

### "Can you customize it?"
Separate configuration from development. Clarify the requested outcome. Never promise unlimited custom development. Price material customization separately or scope it into an enterprise proposal.

### "Can you guarantee results?"
Never guarantee grades, exam scores, pass rates or academic outcomes without rigorous evidence. Sell the workflow, content, implementation and evaluation criteria instead.

## Proposal structure
Use a concise proposal with:
1. Customer context/pain.
2. Recommended ECHS scope.
3. Users/courses/campuses.
4. Pilot or implementation plan.
5. Success criteria.
6. Commercial term.
7. Price/payment schedule.
8. Support/onboarding included.
9. Assumptions/exclusions.
10. Expiry or decision date when commercially justified.
11. Clear next step.

## Three-option proposal
When helpful:
- Core: minimum viable scope.
- Recommended: target scope and best value.
- Expanded: broader school/network deployment.

Do not create fake anchoring. Each option must be genuinely deliverable.

## Closing signals
Move to a close when the buyer asks about:
- price,
- procurement,
- contract term,
- user count,
- implementation date,
- invoicing/payment,
- data/security review,
- pilot activation,
- internal approval materials.

## Closing questions
Use one appropriate question:
- Shall I set up the pilot for the agreed group?
- Would you like the annual department option or the wider school option?
- If I send the proposal today, is there anything else required for approval?
- Are we aligned on scope and commercial terms so I can send the payment/contract next step?

## No endless follow-up
For cold outreach: initial message + one value-added follow-up + one final close-the-loop message maximum unless the prospect re-engages.
For active opportunities: follow up according to the buyer's agreed timeline and procurement process.

## Close-won handoff
Immediately record:
- exact scope,
- price/currency,
- term,
- billing schedule,
- users/campuses,
- start date,
- onboarding owner,
- renewal date,
- promised configuration/support,
- expansion opportunities.

## Close-lost learning
Record one primary reason:
NO FIT / PRICE / TIMING / COMPETITOR / NO BUDGET / NO AUTHORITY / PROCUREMENT / PRODUCT GAP / NO RESPONSE / OTHER.
Use the data to improve targeting, pricing and product roadmap.

## Ethical rule
Win deals through fit, evidence and execution. Never misrepresent ECHS, create fake urgency, impersonate a third party, or pressure a prospect after a clear refusal.
