Checkpoint route · Topics 1.1–1.6

Choose a question group

Use the first question to check your understanding and the second to transfer it to a new example.

Models and motion · 1–8

Polynomial limits, horizontal position graphs, local speed estimates, and linear motion.

Limit notation · 9–12

Write nearby behavior correctly and distinguish a limit from a point value.

Limits from graphs · 13–18

Open and filled points, left and right approaches, and jumps.

Limits from tables · 19–24

Estimate limits, identify one-sided evidence, and recognize what finite data cannot establish.

One-sided limits and limit laws · 25–30

Select a piecewise branch, combine graphical limits, and evaluate trigonometric and exponential expressions.

Equivalent expressions · 31–38

Use conjugates, common factors, trigonometric identities, and common denominators.

Limit laws in new situations · 39–44

Recover unknown limits, analyze absolute values by side, and follow composite functions.

Tangent slopes and motion · 45–56

Choose nearby secants, read speed from line segments, and investigate centered slopes of quadratic models.

Written responses · Sets 1–2

Justify finite limits, apply limit laws, and estimate a local rate from data. Save drafts and check your reasoning against a rubric.

Reference

Course connections

This checkpoint revisits change and instantaneous motion (Topic 1.1), limit notation (1.2), graphical limits (1.3), numerical limits (1.4), limit laws including composite functions (1.5), and equivalent expressions using factoring, conjugates, common denominators, and trigonometric identities (1.6). It is a checkpoint placed after Lesson 1.6, not an additional numbered AP topic.

The questions, models, tables, graphs, and solutions are original classroom practice. They are not official AP exam questions.
