A compact way to record scale

For positive quantities, keep the coefficient between 1 and 10.

a\times10^k,\quad 1\le a<10,\quad k\in\mathbb Z

The coefficient a carries the significant digits; the integer exponent k locates the scale.

5.24 × 106

5.24 million = 5 240 000. A positive exponent often represents a large magnitude; a negative exponent represents a number between 0 and 1 when the coefficient is normalized.

Check the value and the form

48\times10^{-4}=4.8\times10^{-3}=0.0048.

The first expression has the right value but is not normalized. The second is normalized because 1\le4.8<10.

Zero is written as 0. For a negative quantity, keep a minus sign outside the normalized positive magnitude.

On a calculator, 4.8E−3 means 4.8\times10^{-3}. Write mathematical scientific notation in your final answer.

Choose the correct operation rule

Multiplication and division use exponent laws; addition and subtraction need a common power.

Multiply or divide

(7.2\times10^8)(4.5\times10^{-3})=32.4\times10^5=3.24\times10^6.

Multiply coefficients, add exponents, then normalize.

\dfrac{8.4\times10^{-5}}{2\times10^3}=4.2\times10^{-8}.

Divide coefficients and subtract the entire denominator exponent.

Add or subtract

4.2\times10^6+7.5\times10^5

=(4.2+0.75)\times10^6=4.95\times10^6.

Match the powers first. Adding exponents would multiply the scale and gives the wrong operation.

Check the answer against an ordinary-number estimate. Adding two positive quantities must give a result larger than either one.

Decimal places and significant figures answer different questions

Locate the requested final place before looking at the next digit.

Decimal places

Count positions after the decimal point.

6.2048 → 6.20

To 2 decimal places, keep the hundredths place. The next digit is 4, so the hundredths digit stays 0. Write 6.20, including its final zero.

Significant figures

Start counting at the first nonzero digit.

0.004506 → 0.00451

To 3 significant figures, retain 4, 5 and 0. The next digit is 6, so round up to 0.00451.

For these positive quantities, a next digit 5–9 rounds up; 0–4 leaves the retained digit unchanged. Round once from the original value.

Zeros can carry information

Separate leading zeros from measured trailing zeros.

0.005070

The leading zeros locate the decimal point. The digits 5, 0, 7 and final 0 are significant: four significant figures.

Interior zeros count. Trailing zeros after the first nonzero decimal digit count.

Make ambiguous integer zeros explicit

4800 alone may not communicate how many digits are significant.

4.8\times10^3: 2 significant figures.

4.80\times10^3: 3 significant figures.

4.800\times10^3: 4 significant figures.

Use scientific notation when required significant figures would otherwise be unclear.

Estimate first; calculate accurately afterward

A deliberately rough calculation can expose an unreasonable result.

Estimate \dfrac{19.8(0.049)}{0.102} by rounding each input to one significant figure.

19.8\approx20,\quad0.049\approx0.05,\quad0.102\approx0.1.

\dfrac{20(0.05)}{0.1}=10

The calculator result is about 9.51176, so an answer near 10 is reasonable. An answer near 0.1 or 1000 needs checking.

Two different purposes

Estimation: round deliberately to get a quick scale or plausibility check.

Accurate calculation: use the supplied values, keep full intermediate precision, and round only the final answer.

Show the simple numbers used for an estimate. A full calculator result rounded at the end does not show the requested estimation method.

A rounded number describes a range

Find the rounding unit, then move half a unit in each direction.

A length is reported as 8.4 cm to the nearest 0.1 cm.

The rounding unit is 0.1 cm, so half a unit is 0.05 cm.

8.35\le L<8.45

8.35 rounds to 8.4 under the stated positive-number rounding convention. 8.45 rounds to 8.5, so it is excluded.

From significant figures to a unit

3.60 kg to three significant figures ends at the hundredths place. Its rounding unit is 0.01 kg.

3.595\le m<3.605.

750 mL to the nearest 10 mL has rounding unit 10 mL:

745\le V<755.

The upper bound is a boundary; it need not be a possible value. Use an open circle and a strict inequality when it is excluded.

Use endpoint combinations deliberately

For positive measurements, choose inputs that make the calculated quantity smallest or largest.

An area example

8.35\le L<8.45, 3.15\le W<3.25, in cm.

Since both are positive, area A=LW increases with either measurement.

A_{\rm lower}=8.35(3.15)=26.3025.

A_{\rm upper}=8.45(3.25)=27.4625.

26.3025\le A<27.4625\text{ cm}^2

A quotient example

For positive distance d and time t, speed v=d/t is smallest with a small distance and a large time.

v_{\rm lower}=d_{\rm lower}/t_{\rm upper}, v_{\rm upper}=d_{\rm upper}/t_{\rm lower}.

For a difference A − B, use the smallest A and largest B for the lower boundary.

Determine whether an endpoint is attainable. An excluded input endpoint can make a calculated lower endpoint excluded too. Keep exact fractions when stating strict bounds.

Choose the reference before dividing

Percentage error compares an approximation with a stated exact or accepted reference value.

\text{percentage error}=\frac{|v_A-v_E|}{|v_E|}\times100\%

v_A is the approximate value and v_E\ne0 is the exact or specified reference value.

An object has an exact mass of 80 g. A scale reads 78.6 g.

Absolute error: |78.6-80|=1.4 g.

Percentage error: \frac{1.4}{80}\times100\%=1.75\%.

Three checks

  1. Use an absolute difference so the error is nonnegative.
  2. Divide by the stated reference value, not by whichever number is smaller.
  3. Multiply by 100 and include the percent sign.

An equal-sized overestimate of 81.4 g has the same error relative to 80 g.

A measured value is not automatically an exact reference. If a question defines a model reference, say that the error is relative to that model.

A response checklist

  • Write the operation or formula with the actual values.
  • Retain guard digits during the calculation.
  • Give an exact answer or, unless the question specifies otherwise, a final numerical answer to three significant figures.
  • Include units, inequality symbols, and mathematical scientific notation where appropriate.
  • Answer the contextual question: reasonable, within tolerance, or guaranteed?

Accuracy and precision are different

Precision: how finely the measurement is reported. A reading of 4.60 cm is reported to a finer place than 4.6 cm.

Accuracy: closeness to the true or accepted value. Extra displayed zeros do not prove better accuracy.

Bounds describe uncertainty from rounding. Percentage error compares a value with a specified reference. Keep those interpretations distinct.
