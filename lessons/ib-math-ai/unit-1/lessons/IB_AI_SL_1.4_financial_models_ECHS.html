A lump sum

USD 5000 is invested now. It earns 4.5% nominal annually, compounded monthly, for 3 years. No more money is added.

PV=5000,\quad r=4.5,\quad k=12,\quad t=3,\quad N=36

The 36th month-end is the valuation date. A single deposit has PMT = 0.

The compound-interest model

Connect a geometric sequence to a financial value.

Given formula

FV=PV\left(1+\frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kt}

  • PV: initial amount.
  • r: nominal annual rate, as a percentage.
  • k: compounding periods per year.
  • t: time in years; N = kt.

If using an annual factor, the value at time 0 is PV and the year-n value is PV times the factor to power n.

Interest versus balance

Final balance includes the deposit. Interest earned is final balance minus initial deposit.

I=FV-PV

Simple interest may introduce the idea: it adds the same amount each year. Compound interest acts on the updated balance. You are not asked to derive the compound-interest formula in this lesson’s IB-style tasks.

Annual reducing-balance depreciation

Apply the loss percentage to the current value each year.

Model and reverse calculations

V_n=V_0(1-d/100)^n

V₀ is the original asset value, d is the annual depreciation percentage and n counts years.

To find an earlier value, divide by the retention factor for the required years. To find d, solve the decay equation using technology.

Total loss or one year’s loss?

\text{Total loss}=V_0-V_n

\text{Loss in year }n=V_{n-1}-V_n

Constant percentage depreciation gives decreasing annual monetary losses. The model does not imply that an asset’s real selling price is guaranteed.

Real value measures purchasing power

Use the same duration for investment growth and price growth.

Model in today’s money

R=\frac{FV}{(1+f/100)^t}

f is the annual inflation percentage. Divide the future nominal balance by the price-level factor to express it in today’s dollars.

R=PV\frac{(1+r/(100k))^{kt}}{(1+f/100)^t}

Rate differences are approximations

\text{Real annual factor}=\frac{\text{investment annual factor}}{1+f/100}

For 4% annual investment growth and 6% inflation, the real annual change is about −1.89%. Simply subtracting 6% from 4% gives an approximation.

A rising nominal balance can still have falling purchasing power.

From a lump sum to regular payments

SL 1.7 adds amortization and annuities using technology.

A loan timeline

At time 0, the borrower receives the loan. During each period, interest is charged. At the end of that period, a repayment reduces the balance.

B_{n+1}=B_n(1+i)-A

Here i is the periodic decimal rate and A is the positive payment amount. Use this relationship to understand the schedule; Finance Solver or a table handles the calculation.

Technology setup

For a loan fully repaid after N payments: PV is positive, PMT is negative, FV=0, and PmtAt=END.

I% is the nominal annual percentage. Set PpY and CpY to match the context. All assessed regular payments in this lesson are at the end of the period.

Annuities: equal payments at regular intervals

Identify the first payment and the valuation date.

Savings annuity

An initially empty account receives equal deposits at each period-end. In Finance Solver, PV=0, PMT is negative and FV is positive.

Immediately after the Nth deposit, the last deposit has earned no interest. Earlier deposits have earned different numbers of periods of interest.

Withdrawal annuity

An initial fund supports equal withdrawals at each period-end. Using the saver’s viewpoint: PV is negative, PMT is positive. If the fund is exhausted just after the last withdrawal, FV=0.

Use technology to find PV, PMT, FV or N. The annuity formula and its derivation are not required in these assessment tasks.

Why timing changes the balance

Apply interest before each end-of-period deposit.

Two deposits

Deposit USD 200 at each month-end, with a monthly rate of 0.5%, into an empty account.

After deposit 1: USD 200. After deposit 2:

200(1.005)+200=401

The first deposit earns one month’s interest; the second has just arrived. This is the END timing used in the merged lesson’s IB-style tasks.

Worked example: save towards a target

Use Finance Solver to find the regular deposit.

A four-year target

An empty account must reach USD 12000 after 48 month-end deposits, at 4.2% nominal annually compounded monthly.

TI-Nspire: N=48, I%=4.2, PV=0, FV=12000, PpY=CpY=12, END. Solve PMT and interpret the negative result as the deposit paid each month.

Total deposited = 48 × unrounded deposit amount. Interest earned = 12000 − total deposited.

A withdrawal fund

For 60 end-of-month withdrawals of USD 1200 at 3.6% nominal annually compounded monthly, ending with zero balance: N=60, I%=3.6, PMT=1200, FV=0, PpY=CpY=12, END. Solve PV.

The fund required now is the positive amount corresponding to the negative PV output. It can be less than the total withdrawn because the remaining fund earns interest.

A fractional N needs interpretation

A final loan payment may be smaller than the regular payment.

Technology method

  1. Set the loan PV, regular PMT, I%, frequencies and FV=0. Solve N.
  2. If N is not an integer, find the balance after the last full regular payment.
  3. Apply one further period of interest.
  4. Pay that remaining amount at the next payment date.
  5. Use the actual payments to calculate total interest.

Check the wording

“First time the savings balance reaches a target” and “last loan repayment” both require attention to payment dates. A rounded model time is not automatically the correct practical answer.

Question c04 provides a loan with a specified regular payment and an explicit smaller final payment. The method stays within technology-based amortization.

Explain the calculator output

Use this exit discussion before attempting written tasks.

Three claims to audit

  1. “N=48 means 48 years.”
  2. “PMT=−200 means a negative payment amount.”
  3. “The last month-end savings deposit earns a full month of interest immediately.”

Correct each claim with a timeline, units or a cash-flow viewpoint.

Three reliable checks

  1. Convert periods to years using PpY.
  2. Interpret a negative PMT as money paid out.
  3. At END timing, the last deposit earns no interest at the instant it is made.
