Unit 1 Teacher Guide

Number and Algebra · consolidated release 6.0.0

Six-lesson architecture

Number foundations are integrated

Lesson 1.1 combines number sets, precision, rounding, bounds, percentage error and scientific notation so uncertainty is taught where numerical reporting begins.

Sequences develop two kinds of change

Lessons 1.2 and 1.3 contrast constant differences with constant ratios and connect terms, finite sums, thresholds, inverse problems and model limitations.

Financial mathematics has a core-first route

Lesson 1.4 defaults to compound interest, annual depreciation, loan repayments and amortization while retaining clearly labelled reference-supported extensions.

Exponent laws lead naturally to logarithms

Lesson 1.5 begins with exact exponent structure, then develops common-base equations, inverse logarithmic reasoning, contextual thresholds and logarithmic scales.

Technology completes the algebra pathway

Lesson 1.6 is a four-block pathway through systems, parameter fitting, polynomial roots, intersections, residuals, constraints and model verification.

Stable access is preserved

Canonical lesson URLs, authenticated question-bank routing, skill keys and mastery integration remain unchanged.

Instructional design contract

Every screen has one purpose

Launch a need, diagnose prior knowledge, develop one idea, model reasoning, invite transfer, repair a misconception, verify technology, synthesize or assess.

Evidence precedes mastery

Viewing screens is not sufficient. Mastery depends on independent Practice Studio, Timed Quiz and extended-response evidence.

Technology is transparent

Students record the mathematical model, equation or coefficient entry, degree, variable order, graph window or initial guess, output, independent check, units, restrictions and interpretation.

Solutions expose decisions

Worked examples identify why a method applies, where restrictions enter and how the result is checked in every original equation or condition.

Lesson 1.4 multi-day route

1.4A · Percentage change and financial growth

Contrast fixed monetary change with repeated percentage change and establish cash-flow direction.

1.4B · Compounding and rate conventions

Separate nominal annual, periodic and effective annual rates and synchronize time units.

1.4C · Depreciation, inflation and real value

Teach annual reducing-balance depreciation as core; use inflation and real value as labelled extension.

1.4D · Regular deposits and savings

Use as reference-supported extension after core mastery.

1.4E · Loans and repayment

Use TVM variables, repayment calculations and amortization evidence.

1.4F · Financial decision making

Compare strategies using timing, fees, total interest, access and risk.

1.4G · Mastery and mixed applications

Use independent practice, focused IB tasks and quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge routes.

Lesson 1.5 multi-day route

1.5A · Exponent laws and exact powers

  • Develop product, quotient and power laws from repeated multiplication.
  • Interpret zero and negative exponents structurally.
  • Evaluate rational exponents exactly and enforce real-domain restrictions.
  • Repair invalid distribution claims with counterexamples.

1.5B · Exponential equations and common bases

  • Make bases equivalent before comparing exponents.
  • Distinguish unique, inconsistent and identity equations.
  • Use exponential substitution for equations quadratic in a power.
  • Check every candidate in the original equation.

1.5C · Logarithms and model inversion

  • Define logarithms as inverse exponents.
  • Use common and natural logarithms and change of base.
  • Apply logarithm laws only to valid structures.
  • Solve growth and decay equations and verify by substitution or graph intersection.

1.5D · Scales, modelling and mastery

  • Interpret equal scale differences as equal multiplicative ratios.
  • Apply logarithmic structure to selected scale contexts.
  • Reconstruct exponential models from data and evaluate assumptions.
  • Complete five IB-style tasks and independent mastery evidence.

Lesson 1.6 multi-day route

1.6A · Two-variable systems and classification

  • Define a common solution as an ordered pair satisfying both equations.
  • Connect unique, inconsistent and dependent systems with line geometry.
  • Compare graph, elimination and simultaneous-equation routes.
  • Require substitution into every original equation.

1.6B · Three variables, parameter fitting and feasibility

  • Keep a fixed variable order and enter zero coefficients explicitly.
  • Solve 3×3 systems and interpret the common triple.
  • Fit linear and quadratic parameters from independent conditions.
  • Apply non-negativity, integrality, capacity and feasibility constraints.

1.6C · Polynomial roots, intersections and numerical solving

  • Connect roots, zeros and x-intercepts.
  • Use degree and multiplicity to audit completeness.
  • Solve \(f(x)=g(x)\) through intersections or a zero function.
  • Use broad windows and multiple initial guesses to avoid hidden roots.

1.6D · Verification, constraints, modelling and mastery

  • Calculate residuals and compare them with an appropriate tolerance.
  • Retain guard digits and apply discrete reporting rules explicitly.
  • Separate algebraic validity from contextual admissibility.
  • Complete five IB tasks, a 14-question quiz and evidence-based mastery review.

Lesson 1.6 official scope

The core syllabus focus is SL 1.8: use technology to solve polynomial equations and systems of linear equations in up to three variables. Exact factorization and elimination are used as verification and structural explanation, not as a replacement for the required transparent technology habit.

Lesson 1.6 technology evidence

Systems record

  • Define variables, units and restrictions.
  • Write every equation before using the solver.
  • Record variable order and zero coefficients.
  • Substitute the full ordered pair or triple into every condition.

Polynomial-root record

  • Enter coefficients in descending degree order.
  • Record the complete real-root output.
  • Use degree and multiplicity to audit completeness.
  • Verify by substitution, factorization or a graph.

Intersection record

  • State the two functions or the equivalent zero equation.
  • Record the graph window or initial guess.
  • Scan broadly before numerical refinement.
  • Compare both sides at the reported solution.

Context and precision

  • State domain, integrality and non-negativity restrictions.
  • Retain guard digits until the final answer.
  • Use residuals relative to the reporting precision.
  • Verify adjacent values for whole-period or threshold decisions.

Daily lesson use

Before class

  • Choose a stopping point in the slide map.
  • Preview the Student Turn and likely misconception.
  • Decide whether technology is teacher-led or student-led.

During class

  • Do not reveal a Student Turn before students commit.
  • Require settings, coefficient order and restrictions in explanations.
  • Use incorrect entries and poor graph windows as misconception evidence.

After class

  • Assign the Practice Studio level matching the evidence need.
  • Use quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge routes.
  • Reserve IB Tasks for independent or partially scaffolded evidence.

Assessment integrity

  • Practice, quiz and extended tasks are separate routes.
  • Worked solutions support learning but are not mastery evidence.
  • A calculator output without setup, verification and interpretation is incomplete.
