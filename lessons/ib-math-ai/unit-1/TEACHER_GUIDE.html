Number and Algebra · release 5.3.2

Instructional design contract

Every screen must do one job

Launch a need, expose prior knowledge, develop one idea, model reasoning, invite transfer, repair a misconception, verify technology, synthesize or assess.

Evidence before mastery

Viewing screens is not sufficient. Mastery depends on independent Practice Studio, quiz and extended-response evidence.

Technology is transparent

Students record the model, entries or settings, relevant output, units, verification and interpretation. A screenshot or decimal alone is not a complete solution.

Source-informed, independently written

Pearson, Haese and Nikolaidis materials establish scope, terminology and task patterns. ECHS explanations, contexts, visuals and assessment prompts are independently written.

Lesson 1.2 multi-day route

Day 1 · Language and invariant

  • Separate sequence, term, series and partial sum.
  • Use first-difference tables to identify arithmetic structure.
  • Require a formula-based proof for infinite sequences.

Day 2 · Representations

  • Move among recursive, explicit, tabular and graphical forms.
  • Build the \(n-1\) jump argument before quoting the formula.
  • Use Student Turns before revealing modelled solutions.

Day 3 · Terms and parameters

  • Find distant terms and solve backwards for an index.
  • Reject non-integer indices when a target is not a term.
  • Recover \(u_1\) and \(d\) from separated or symbolic terms.

Day 4 · Series and sigma

  • Expand and compress sigma notation.
  • Count \(n-m+1\) terms for shifted limits.
  • Derive the arithmetic-series formula by first–last pairing.

Day 5 · Inverse and threshold problems

  • Find term count from a last term.
  • Find least \(n\) for a cumulative threshold.
  • Recover \(u_n\) from \(S_n-S_{n-1}\).

Day 6–7 · Modelling and evidence

  • Use the interactive sequence explorer and generative studio.
  • Evaluate constant-change assumptions and residuals.
  • Use Practice Studio, a focused IB task and the independent quiz.

Daily lesson use

Before class

  • Choose a stopping point in the slide map.
  • Preview the Student Turn and likely misconception.
  • Decide whether technology is teacher-led or student-led.

During class

  • Do not reveal a Student Turn before students commit.
  • Use incorrect responses as evidence for the misconception clinic.
  • Require index, units and interpretation in contextual examples.

After class

  • Assign a targeted Practice Studio level rather than every item.
  • Use quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge routes.
  • Reserve IB Tasks for independent or partially scaffolded evidence.

Assessment integrity

  • Practice, quiz and extended tasks are separate routes.
  • Worked solutions support learning but are not mastery evidence.
  • Technology output must be accompanied by model, settings and validation.
