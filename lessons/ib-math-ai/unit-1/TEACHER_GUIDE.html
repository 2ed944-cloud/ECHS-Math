Unit 1 Teacher Guide

Number and Algebra · six-lesson teaching route

Six-lesson architecture

Scientific notation and uncertainty begin the route

Lesson 1.1 starts with normalized scientific notation, scale and operations, then develops significant figures, rounding, bounds and percentage error. Number-set material remains available as prerequisite review rather than controlling the default classroom path.

Sequences develop two kinds of change

Lessons 1.2 and 1.3 contrast constant differences with constant ratios and connect terms, finite sums, thresholds, inverse problems and model limitations.

Financial mathematics has a core-first route

Lesson 1.4 begins with compound interest, annual depreciation, loan repayments and amortization while retaining reference-supported enrichment for later use.

Exponent laws lead naturally to logarithms

Lesson 1.5 begins with exact exponent structure, then develops common-base equations, inverse logarithmic reasoning, contextual thresholds and logarithmic scales.

Technology completes the algebra pathway

Lesson 1.6 moves through systems, parameter fitting, polynomial roots, intersections, residuals, constraints and model verification.

Access remains stable

Canonical lesson URLs, authenticated question-bank routing, skill keys and mastery integration remain unchanged.

Lesson 1.1 calculator use

Use technology only where it adds value

Students should first predict sign, exponent and approximate scale. The calculator is then used to enter scientific notation accurately, preserve guard digits and verify a result—not to replace the mathematical structure.

Three classroom workflows

  • A1: enter EE notation and distinguish the negative key from subtraction.
  • A2: compare SCI and NORMAL displays while recognizing that the stored value is unchanged.
  • B1: use brackets for a quotient, keep guard digits and round once at the end.

TI‑84 Classroom

Use Teacher demo, Students follow and Exam drill modes to model the physical key sequence, independent estimate and complete IB-style statement.

Self-contained simulator

The lesson simulator is built into ECHS Mathematics and does not depend on an external website or iframe. It supports the three Lesson 1.1 workflows and remains separate from mastery evidence.

Instructional design contract

Every screen has one purpose

Launch a need, diagnose prior knowledge, develop one idea, model reasoning, invite transfer, repair a misconception, verify technology, synthesize or assess.

Evidence precedes mastery

Viewing screens is not sufficient. Mastery depends on independent Practice Studio, Timed Quiz and extended-response evidence.

Technology is transparent

Students record the mathematical model or calculator entry, output, independent check, units, restrictions and interpretation.

Solutions expose decisions

Worked examples identify why a method applies, where restrictions enter and how the result is checked in the original conditions.

Technology evidence

  • Define variables and restrictions.
  • Record coefficient or variable order.
  • Show the exact entry and complete output.
  • Verify with substitution, residuals, factorization, estimation or a second representation.

Daily lesson use

Before class

  • Choose a stopping point in the slide map.
  • Preview the Student Turn and likely misconception.
  • Decide whether calculator use is teacher-led or student-led.

During class

  • Do not reveal a Student Turn before students commit.
  • Require settings, coefficient order and restrictions in explanations.
  • Use incorrect entries and poor graph windows as misconception evidence.

After class

  • Assign the Practice Studio level matching the evidence need.
  • Use quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge routes.
  • Reserve IB Tasks for independent or partially scaffolded evidence.

Assessment integrity

  • Practice, quiz and extended tasks are separate routes.
  • Worked solutions support learning but are not mastery evidence.
  • A calculator output without setup, verification and interpretation is incomplete.
