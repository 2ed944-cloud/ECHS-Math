Instructional design contract

Every screen must do one job

Launch a need, expose prior knowledge, develop one idea, model reasoning, invite transfer, repair a misconception, verify technology, synthesize or assess.

Evidence before mastery

Viewing screens is not sufficient. Mastery depends on independent Practice Studio, quiz and extended-response evidence.

Technology is transparent

Students record the model, entries or settings, relevant output, units, verification and interpretation. A screenshot or decimal alone is not a complete solution.

Source-informed, independently written

The supplied Pearson, Haese, Nikolaidis and MAI materials establish scope and terminology. Explanations, contexts, visuals and assessment prompts are original ECHS adaptations.

Lesson 1.1 v6 teaching route

Number Foundations, Scientific Notation and Approximation

The expanded lesson is designed for a multi-day route. It moves through number sets, decimal places, significant figures, rounding, exact values and approximations, direct and calculated bounds, percentage error, then scientific notation and order-of-magnitude validation.

Recommended pacing

  • Periods 1–2: Number sets, exact values and representations.
  • Periods 3–4: Decimal places, significant figures and rounding.
  • Periods 5–6: Approximation, error intervals and calculated bounds.
  • Period 7: Percentage error, tolerance and modelling judgement.
  • Periods 8–9: Scientific notation, operations, units and scale.
  • Period 10: IB task evidence, quiz and targeted remediation.

Non-negotiable checkpoint: students must distinguish value from representation, preserve stated precision, select bound endpoints deliberately, use the accepted value in percentage-error calculations and validate scientific-notation output independently.

Daily lesson use

Before class

  • Choose the intended stopping point in the slide map.
  • Preview the Student Turn and likely misconception.
  • Decide whether the interactive map, bounds explorer or generator is teacher-led or student-led.

During class

  • Do not reveal a Student Turn before students commit to reasoning.
  • Use incorrect responses as evidence for the misconception clinic.
  • Require sets, units, precision and interpretation where relevant.

After class

  • Assign a targeted Practice Studio level rather than all questions indiscriminately.
  • Use generated questions for short retrieval and immediate feedback.
  • Use quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge next actions.

Assessment integrity

  • Practice, quiz and extended tasks are separate routes.
  • Worked solutions support learning but are not mastery evidence.
  • Record calculator methods, bounds and assumptions, not only outputs.
