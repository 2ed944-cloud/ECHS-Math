Revised six-lesson architecture

Approximation is no longer isolated

Decimal places, significant figures, rounding, bounds and percentage error are taught inside Lesson 1.1, where precision and uncertainty directly support scientific notation and numerical interpretation.

Financial mathematics is one coherent route

Lesson 1.4 now joins financial growth, depreciation, inflation, annuities, loans and amortization through dated cash flows and repeated multiplicative change.

Technology is the final lesson

Technology for Equations and Systems is renumbered as Lesson 1.6. Legacy Lesson 1.8 links redirect to the new canonical route.

Legacy access is preserved

Old Approximation and Loans lesson URLs redirect to Lessons 1.1 and 1.4, so saved links do not produce dead pages.

Instructional design contract

Every screen must do one job

Launch a need, expose prior knowledge, develop one idea, model reasoning, invite transfer, repair a misconception, verify technology, synthesize or assess.

Evidence before mastery

Viewing screens is not sufficient. Mastery depends on independent Practice Studio, quiz and extended-response evidence.

Technology is transparent

Students record the model, timeline, rate convention, settings, output, independent check, units and interpretation.

Source-informed, independently written

Pearson, Haese and Nikolaidis materials establish scope, terminology and task patterns. ECHS explanations, contexts, visuals and assessment prompts are independently written.

Lesson 1.4 multi-day route

Day 1 · Timelines and growth structure

  • Establish valuation date and cash-flow direction.
  • Contrast simple interest as arithmetic change with compound interest as geometric change.
  • Use balance sequences before formulas.

Day 2 · Rate conventions

  • Separate nominal annual, periodic and effective rates.
  • Synchronize rate, payment and time units.
  • Compare compounding frequencies using EAR.

Day 3 · Inverse growth and decay

  • Solve amount, time and rate questions.
  • Verify completed-period thresholds.
  • Model reducing-balance depreciation.

Day 4 · Inflation and real value

  • Distinguish nominal balance from purchasing power.
  • Use exact real growth factors.
  • Compare plans at one date after inflation and fees.

Day 5 · Ordinary annuities

  • Derive future value from a geometric sum.
  • Separate contributions from interest.
  • Combine a lump sum with regular deposits.

Day 6 · Timing and withdrawal funds

  • Compare ordinary annuity and annuity due.
  • Solve savings targets with adjacent payment counts.
  • Use present value for retirement withdrawals.

Day 7 · Loans and TVM

  • Record N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y and END/BEGIN.
  • Derive payment from annuity present value.
  • Calculate total repayment and total interest.

Day 8 · Amortization and balances

  • Build the interest–principal–balance recurrence.
  • Read and validate an amortization schedule.
  • Reconcile retrospective and prospective balances.

Day 9 · Strategy and sensitivity

  • Analyse extra payments and loan-term tradeoffs.
  • Distinguish exact model payments from contractual currency rounding.
  • Test whether recommendations survive plausible changes.

Day 10 · Independent evidence

  • Use the compound and cash-flow explorers.
  • Assign targeted Practice levels.
  • Complete one focused IB task and the independent quiz.

Scope boundary

The final growing-annuity screen is optional enrichment drawn from the broader loans-and-annuities resource sequence. Fixed-payment annuities, loans and amortization form the core assessed route; growing annuities are excluded from Practice Studio, Timed Quiz and IB Tasks.

Financial technology evidence

Finance solver

  • State the chosen viewpoint.
  • Record N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y and END/BEGIN.
  • Explain the signs of PV, PMT and FV.
  • Interpret the solved quantity in context.

Spreadsheet or recurrence

  • Label opening balance, interest, payment, principal and closing balance.
  • Show the first recurrence explicitly.
  • Check that interest + principal = payment.
  • Check that opening − principal = closing.

Threshold evidence

  • Record the continuous or solver boundary.
  • Select an admissible completed period.
  • Show the preceding and reported values.
  • State why the reported period is first.

Decision evidence

  • Compare net values on one valuation date.
  • Use real value when inflation matters.
  • Include fees, timing and access assumptions.
  • Perform at least one sensitivity test.

Daily lesson use

Before class

  • Choose a stopping point in the slide map.
  • Preview the Student Turn and likely misconception.
  • Decide whether technology is teacher-led or student-led.

During class

  • Do not reveal a Student Turn before students commit.
  • Require timelines before complex financial calculations.
  • Use incorrect settings as evidence for misconception repair.

After class

  • Assign a targeted Practice Studio level rather than every item.
  • Use quiz evidence to select support, core or challenge routes.
  • Reserve IB Tasks for independent or partially scaffolded evidence.

Assessment integrity

  • Practice, quiz and extended tasks are separate routes.
  • Worked solutions support learning but are not mastery evidence.
  • A calculator output without settings and verification is incomplete.
