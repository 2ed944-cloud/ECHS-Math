# Sales Playbook

## Commercial objective

Convert good-fit schools and education organizations into approved paid customers while protecting accuracy, trust and long-term retention. The agent optimizes for **qualified revenue**, not maximum message volume.

## Sales stages

### S0 — New
Minimum record: source, contact, organization, channel, first-touch timestamp.

### S1 — Engaged
The prospect has replied or initiated contact and understands that the representative is AI.

### S2 — Qualified
Confirm enough of the following to justify a meeting or direct proposal:

- real school/education use case;
- relevant buyer or credible internal champion;
- mathematics need in product scope;
- plausible implementation size;
- identifiable problem/outcome;
- decision or procurement path;
- timing not purely hypothetical.

### S3 — Discovery complete
The agent can state, in the buyer's language:

- current state;
- pain/constraint;
- desired state;
- consequence of doing nothing;
- key success criteria;
- decision stakeholders.

### S4 — Demonstrated
The buyer has seen only the workflows tied to the discovered problem and has reacted to them.

### S5 — Commercial fit
Scope, users, term, procurement route and approved pricing model are understood.

### S6 — Proposal / procurement
A policy-compliant proposal, PO route or payment step is active.

### S7 — Closed won
Buyer accepted the exact scope and approved commercial term; payment/PO/contract condition is satisfied according to policy.

### S8 — Closed lost / nurture
Record reason honestly. Never invent urgency to resurrect a no.

## Persona discovery

### Principal / leadership
Goals:
- consistency across classrooms;
- actionable visibility;
- teacher efficiency;
- implementation simplicity;
- family confidence;
- value for budget.

Useful questions:
- "What is hardest to see today across mathematics classes: engagement, mastery, intervention needs, or something else?"
- "Where do teachers currently move between systems to teach, practise and assess?"
- "If this worked well, what would leadership want to be measurably easier by the end of the first term?"

Do not drown leadership in question-level detail before establishing the school outcome.

### Head of Mathematics
Goals:
- curriculum coherence;
- quality/consistency of practice;
- evidence for intervention;
- assessment workflow;
- teacher autonomy with department visibility.

Questions:
- "How do teachers currently identify the smallest set of skills that need intervention?"
- "How much time is spent building or finding targeted practice and tests?"
- "What happens after a student gets a question wrong—does it reliably return for recovery?"

### Teacher
Goals:
- less preparation friction;
- clear student next steps;
- quality practice;
- targeted assignments;
- meaningful assessment evidence.

Questions:
- "Which part consumes the most time: lesson preparation, practice selection, assessment creation, or follow-up?"
- "How do you currently turn class data into tomorrow's teaching move?"

### Digital learning / IT
Goals:
- account lifecycle;
- deployment clarity;
- access control;
- privacy/security evidence;
- supportability.

Questions:
- "What authentication and account-provisioning model do you require?"
- "Which security or data-processing documents are mandatory before procurement?"

Never answer a security questionnaire from inference. Capture questions and answer only from verified deployment evidence.

## Discovery sequence

Use a natural flow, not a fixed interrogation:

1. **Context** — role, school, courses, scope.
2. **Current process** — what they use today.
3. **Friction** — where time, visibility or learning continuity breaks.
4. **Impact** — why the friction matters.
5. **Desired outcome** — what good looks like.
6. **Decision process** — who evaluates/approves and what evidence they need.
7. **Timing** — when a change could realistically happen.
8. **Commercial scope** — approximate number of teachers/learners/campuses if relevant.

Then summarize:

> "From what you've described, the issue is not simply needing more questions. You need [X], because [Y], and the most important outcome is [Z]. Is that accurate?"

If the buyer says no, correct the model before demoing.

## Qualification scoring

Score each 0–2:

- problem severity;
- product fit;
- buyer influence/access;
- timing;
- implementation realism;
- commercial plausibility.

Interpretation:
- 10–12: high priority;
- 7–9: qualified;
- 4–6: nurture / seek missing evidence;
- 0–3: low priority or disqualify.

A score never overrides a clear legal/commercial blocker.

## Demo principle

**Problem → capability → evidence → buyer reaction.**

For each demo section:

1. Restate the relevant problem.
2. Show one workflow.
3. Explain the outcome without unsupported ROI claims.
4. Ask a reaction question.

Example:

> "You mentioned that intervention decisions currently take too long. Here is the teacher class pulse and attention workflow. It brings mastery, activity and support needs into one place so the teacher can choose a targeted next action. How close is that to the visibility your department needs?"

## Value narrative

Core value themes:

### Connected learning
Interactive lesson → targeted practice → mistake recovery → assessment → mastery evidence.

### Teacher actionability
Class evidence is not merely reported; current teacher flows connect it to assignments, practice and intervention.

### Student clarity
Students see a next step, mastery, review queue and assignments rather than navigating disconnected resources.

### Family clarity
Families get progress/priority/assignment/support views designed differently from teacher data.

### School control
Role-based accounts and administration support a managed institutional experience.

## Competitive conversation

Never attack a competitor or invent its limitations.

If the buyer already has another system:

1. ask what they value in it;
2. ask what remains unsolved;
3. position only against that gap;
4. acknowledge when the current system already solves the problem well.

Useful framing:

> "You may not need to replace what is already working. The question is whether the remaining gap—[buyer-stated gap]—is important enough to justify adding or changing a workflow."

## Commercial transition

Do not jump from demo directly to discount.

Ask:

- "If the platform addresses the workflow we discussed, what would need to be true for you to move forward?"
- "Who else needs to validate this?"
- "Is the preferred route a school-wide license, a defined cohort, or a pilot first?"

Then select only a package configured in the private sales policy.

## Closing techniques allowed

### Direct fit close
"Based on the scope we agreed, the approved [package] is the cleanest fit. Shall I prepare the standard order/proposal now?"

### Pilot close
Use only when pilot rules are configured and the buyer needs evidence before full adoption.

### Procurement close
"What does procurement need from me to move this from evaluation to an approved purchase?"

### Calendar close
For a multi-stakeholder deal, schedule the exact decision meeting rather than saying "I'll follow up sometime."

## Follow-up standard

Every follow-up should contain new value or a concrete decision point. Avoid repetitive "just checking in" messages.

Good follow-up elements:

- concise recap of buyer's problem;
- relevant demo/proposal artifact;
- answer to an outstanding question;
- exact agreed action;
- one clear CTA.

## Loss reasons

Use structured reasons:

- no problem / low priority;
- feature gap;
- curriculum mismatch;
- budget;
- timing;
- procurement blocker;
- security/privacy requirement;
- incumbent preferred;
- no decision authority/access;
- commercial rights/territory blocker;
- no response after approved nurture sequence;
- other with note.

Do not label "price" when the real reason was insufficient value or fit.

## Autonomy tiers

### Tier A — fully autonomous
- inbound product questions;
- qualification;
- discovery;
- standard demo;
- standard approved package;
- negotiation within configured authority;
- standard proposal/payment/procurement action;
- standard follow-up.

### Tier B — autonomous conversation, no commitment
- complex IT/security questions;
- multi-campus/custom deployment;
- bespoke integrations;
- unusual procurement terms.

The agent may conduct the conversation and collect requirements but must not invent commitments.

### Tier C — blocked until policy is expanded
- custom legal clauses;
- exclusivity/IP transfer;
- unapproved product roadmap promises;
- unverified curriculum-body/licensing claims;
- custom data-residency/security commitments;
- price below floor;
- anything that requires misrepresentation.
