## Mathematics and level

The activity content follows the verified College Board AP Precalculus CED effective Fall 2026, Topic 1.2, with the Topic 1.1 graph/concavity bridge needed by the uploaded items. Basic quadratic vertex knowledge is used as a prerequisite. No derivative notation, differentiation rules, formal limits, trigonometric calculations or function-family finite-difference classification is required.

Key clarifications are explicit:

- The least signed rate is the smallest signed value; it is the most negative when negative candidates occur.
- Concave up does not imply increasing. A negative rate becoming less negative is an increasing signed rate.
- Smooth turning points have local rate zero; a centered interval average and a point rate remain distinct.
- A down-to-up change in concavity can give a local minimum of signed rate in the stated smooth setting; an arbitrary inflection is not automatically a minimum-rate point.
- Rates must refer to the same input interval before they are added.
- Opposing signs permit a constant sum through cancellation, as well as increasing, decreasing or mixed behavior.
- Given signed changes can be summed without knowing initial outputs. Absolute changes answer a different question.
- Whole-interval average rate is total signed change divided by total input width. A simple mean of component rates is appropriate only for equal widths.

All contextual data are illustrative. The visual greenhouse and water models are read graphically; their internal trigonometric formulas are not student prerequisites.
