# IB AI SL Lesson 1.2 — arithmetic sequences and series

The former lesson mixed current AI SL content with non-calculator Paper 1 labels and an oversized proof-oriented practice route. This revision follows official SL 1.2 and matches the existing Lesson 1.1 teaching and written-response format.

## Scope and teaching

- First-assessment-2021 guide for the current pre-2029 cohort: arithmetic sequences, finite series, nth-term and sum formulas, sigma notation, simple interest, and approximate arithmetic modelling.
- Two teaching blocks: 1.2A Arithmetic Sequences and 1.2B Arithmetic Series and Applications. Each is planned for 60–70 minutes with selected checks; teachers select written tasks for subsequent practice.
- 47 slides, eight investigations, twelve immediate-feedback learning checks, eight short tasks, three extended tasks and three SL challenges. The 14 written tasks contain 49 parts worth 100 marks; this is a practice collection, not a mock examination paper.
- Investigations connect first differences, discrete plots, index gaps, paired sums, shifted sigma limits, simple-interest timelines, approximate observations and calculator evidence.
- Both AI SL papers require a GDC. TI-Nspire CX / CX II guidance covers term entry, seq, sum and threshold tables. The local coach checks predictions; it is not an emulator.
- Geometric/infinite series, compound-interest methods and formal induction are outside this lesson. All active challenges remain within SL 1.2.

## Existing platform behavior

The canonical lesson URL, course identity, access parameters and guarded progression are preserved. New responses use a separate account-scoped revision key; older saved work and earlier lesson assets are retained. Written solutions require a draft, marks are self-assessed, and editing a draft clears its review score. Self-assessment does not award mastery. Earlier companion notes are labelled as earlier editions. Printing produces a student copy with hints and solutions hidden.

## Verification

The mathematics test independently checks term and sum calculations, all numerical written-task conclusions, strict and inclusive thresholds, time-zero balances, approximate models, 204 static equations and portal counts. The DOM behavior test checks every investigation, all twelve checks, all 49 variable-mark parts, equation rendering, keyboard navigation, stable hashes, account isolation, unavailable storage, reset, printing and guarded progression. Shared lesson/platform and local-link gates also apply. A deployment verifier checks the published commit, protected lesson wrapper and byte-identical assets.
