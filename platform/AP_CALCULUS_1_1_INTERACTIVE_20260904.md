# AP Calculus Topic 1.1 interactive rebuild

Canonical lesson: `lessons/ap-calculus/unit-1/1-1-can-change-occur-at-an-instant.html`.

## Curriculum scope

Checked against the current College Board AP Calculus AB and BC Course and Exam Description, Topic 1.1 (printed page 33; PDF page 38), on 4 September 2026:
https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-calculus-ab-and-bc-course-and-exam-description.pdf#page=38

The core targets CHA-1.A, CHA-1.A.1–3 and practice 2.B: interpreting instantaneous change through nearby average rates and connecting graphical, numerical, analytical and verbal representations. Limit notation is introduced as a preview. No differentiation rules, epsilon–delta proofs or later calculus theorems are required.

The optional challenges investigate a corner, identical finite data with different local rates, speed versus velocity, and a tangent that crosses a cubic. These deepen reasoning using the same average-rate idea and elementary algebra.

## Teaching and interaction

- 33 navigable HTML slides with six interactive investigations: secants, a bike journey, shrinking free-fall intervals, tangent fitting, a multi-model calculator explorer, and a corner.
- Classification feedback, six guided/numerical questions, 12 original AP-style MCQs and two objective exit questions: 20 objective questions in total. Three multipart FRQs have 9-point classroom rubrics and explicit self-assessment.
- Solutions and rubrics start concealed. MCQ/numeric solutions require a valid attempt; each FRQ rubric requires written work for that part. Explanatory worked examples and challenge solutions use explicit reveal controls.
- Corrected the old bike example's average-speed/average-velocity confusion. Added discrete-domain cautions, accurate compound units, two-sided evidence, and the limitations of estimates from finite tables.
- Reused the local KaTeX renderer. Replaced embedded multi-megabyte photographs with accurate functional graphs and the shared ECHS logo.
- Account-scoped device-local drafts, keyboard-safe navigation, numbered deep links, responsive internal scrolling, focus controls, and a student print view that conceals solutions and rubrics.
- The legacy lesson address redirects to the canonical lesson while preserving course, release, lesson and slide parameters.
- Existing authentication, publication and finish-to-practice actions remain authoritative. Viewing slides, revealing answers and self-assessed marks do not silently award platform mastery.
